AstroArchive 3.2.8 prerelease
Package 3.2.8.1

Choosing or changing a capture town now applies its historical timezone to unzoned local capture clocks and updates the UTC instant used by the sky map and eclipse checks. Existing chosen-town records with stale assumed UTC are repaired when the archive opens. Explicit UTC, clock offsets and declared header time standards retain their meaning. For the Santa Ponsa eclipse example, 12 August 2026 at 20:32:54.797 CEST resolves to 18:32:54.797 UTC, placing the Sun above the western horizon.

Thumbnail credits have been audited and repaired: Commons featured-picture notices, copyright-template instructions and talk-page controls no longer appear as creator or attribution text. Real creator, collaborator, processing and licence information is preserved. The pack exporter applies the same corrections on future rebuilds.

Comet imaging notes suggest 10–30 second sub-exposures, 5–10 seconds for faster motion or longer focal lengths, and give an image-scale/motion calculation for keeping the head sharp. Entries without imaging guidance omit the imaging notes.

The target list now shows one Comets entry instead of every comet separately. Selecting it shows each comet's captures together, and its information page lists your comets; select one to read its page and use All comets to return. Imaging notes are shown in full rather than in a drop-down, surface gravity is given in g alongside m/s², and every information page that has a single target has an Open in Stellarium button (Stellarium Web when Stellarium is not installed). Solar Eclipse and Lunar Eclipse have their own pages with a description, imaging notes and an image. The Sun now shows its full disk, the Elephant's Trunk Nebula has a better image, and NGC 7640 has a Hubble image and is described as a barred spiral galaxy. All new images are credited and licensed like the existing ones.
